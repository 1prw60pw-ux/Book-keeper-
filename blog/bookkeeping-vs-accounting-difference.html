Bookkeeping vs Accounting: Which One Do I Actually Need?

By Patricia Woods · August 11, 2026

Bookkeeping is the day-to-day recording of what your business spends and earns. Accounting is the bigger-picture analysis, tax strategy, and reporting built on top of those records. Most small businesses need consistent bookkeeping all year and only need deeper accounting or CPA-level tax work at specific moments, like filing season or a major business decision.

Here's how to tell which one you're missing, and why the difference actually matters for your wallet.

What does a bookkeeper actually do?

A bookkeeper keeps your financial records accurate and current. That means:

  • Recording every transaction: sales, expenses, transfers, payroll
  • Reconciling your bank and credit card accounts each month
  • Categorizing expenses correctly so your reports mean something
  • Keeping your books in QuickBooks (or whatever software you use) clean and up to date
  • Producing basic reports: profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow

Think of bookkeeping as the ongoing maintenance. It's the difference between knowing exactly where your money went in March and guessing.

What does an accountant do that a bookkeeper doesn't?

An accountant (often a CPA) takes the records a bookkeeper produces and does something with them at a higher level:

  • Prepares and files tax returns
  • Advises on tax strategy: entity structure, deductions, estimated payments
  • Analyzes financial statements for business decisions
  • Represents you if the IRS has questions
  • Signs off on financials for loans, investors, or audits
